# MisoStory map authoring guide

How to build one classic Maple Island map as an isometric 3D space. All geometry is generated by Python modules
run inside Blender 5.2. **Read this whole file before writing a map.** The two reference implementations are
`src/misostory/maps/m10000_MushroomTown.py` (town) and `src/misostory/maps/m40000_SnailHuntingGround1.py`
(hunting ground). Copy their structure.

## 1. The goal

> "What would classic Maple Island feel like if MapleStory had originally been an isometric 3D game?"

Each map must be **recognizably its classic self**: keep its landmarks, NPCs, monsters, its progression and the
experience of moving through it (tiers, ladders, ropes, slopes). Translate the 2D composition intelligently into
3D. Do not extrude the side view, and do not invent a generic field. The look is colorful, whimsical, cozy, chunky
and hand-crafted, with strong silhouettes. Translate original platforms into terraces, embankments, cliffs,
raised paths, rooftops, bridges and natural slopes. Avoid arbitrary floating platforms unless the original
genuinely has them, such as a tiny islet; even then, make them read as rock pillars or stumps.

## 2. Files you own and files you must not touch

- You own **only** `src/misostory/maps/m<MAPID>_<ShortName>.py`, for example `m40001_SnailHuntingGround2.py`.
  The module defines `MAP_ID`, `build(m)`, and optionally `GAMEPLAY_FOCUS = (x, y)`, `GAMEPLAY_SCALE` and
  `OVERVIEW_EXTRA` (§4, "Wave-4 framework additions").
- **Never edit** shared files: `mapkit.py`, `layout.py`, `world.py`, `geo.py`, `materials.py`, `kit/*`,
  `build.py` and `scripts/*`. Other agents are building other maps in parallel.
- If you need a **new asset** that the kit lacks, define it inside your own module with the kit decorator and a
  map-prefixed name:

  ```python
  from misostory.kit import asset
  from misostory.geo import MeshBuilder

  @asset("X60000_Lighthouse", "Architecture", footprint=6.0, tags=("landmark",))
  def _lighthouse(coll):
      mb = MeshBuilder()
      mb.cyl(2.2, 9.0, mat="M_Plaster", verts=20, r2=1.8)
      ...
      mb.build("X60000_Lighthouse", coll, smooth=True, sharp_angle=40)
  ```

  List any such assets in your final report so they can be promoted to the shared kit.
- Read the existing kit in `docs/ASSET_CATALOG.md`, which lists every asset with its footprint. Materials are
  in `src/misostory/materials.py` (`RECIPES` and `PALETTE`). Any palette color can be used as `M_Flat_<key>`,
  `M_Glow_<key>` (emissive), `M_Spot_<base>__<spot>` (spotted) or `M_Grad_<bottom>__<top>` (vertical gradient).

## 3. Space, scale and the camera

- **Local map frame** (meters): `x` is the original left→right progression, from 0 to L. `y` is depth, from
  0 at the front (camera side) to D at the back. `z` is height above the map's base level.
- The world layout (`layout.py`) fixes each map's size `(L, D)`, base height, yaw, and the **anchors**: the local
  `(x, y)` positions of its edge portals. Trails connect anchors between neighbouring maps, so **your
  `edge_portal(...)` positions are fixed by the anchors**. Put walkable ground at each anchor. You choose the
  anchor's height through your terrain; the trail adapts.
- A **plinth** already provides grass ground at local `z = 0` over the whole footprint plus 4 m. Build upward
  from there. Terrace bottoms should reach `0` or below so nothing floats. Stay inside the footprint: terrain
  may overhang the edges by at most about 1 m. Areas lower than 0 are impossible; raise the rest instead.
- **Camera:** orthographic, pitched 40° down, looking toward world +X+Y. Local −y faces are always visible.
  Whether local −x **or +x** faces are visible depends on the map's yaw. `m.view_dir()` returns the camera's
  look direction in local coordinates, and `build_map.py` prints it as `MAP_VIEW_DIR`. A face with local normal
  `n` is visible when `dot(n, view_dir) < 0`. Example: for world yaw −103 (40001) the view direction is about
  (−0.85, +0.53), so **+x** faces are visible and −x faces are hidden. **Higher tiers go further back (+y)** so
  their cliff faces show. A tall thing in the front band hides what is behind it, so keep the front 25% low
  (meadows, paths, low fences, small bushes). Windows and doors accept `face="-Y" | "+X" | "+Y" | "-X"`.
- **Scale:** the player is 1.5 m tall (chibi). Doors are about 2 m and mushroom houses 6–7 m. Paths are at least
  2.5 m wide, main routes 3–4 m. Mob proxies: Snail 0.70 m tall, Blue/Red Snail 0.77 m, Shroom 0.78 m, Orange
  Mushroom 1.22 m (footprints 1.0, 1.05, 0.8 and 1.4 m); give crowded spawn areas a larger `min_dist`.
- **Converting from the 2D data** (`docs/research/maps/<id>.struct.json` and the brief):
  - Horizontal: map the foothold x-extent onto `[0, L]` (about 0.02 m per px).
  - Vertical: a 60 px classic tier is about 0.55–0.7 m (the shared island scale, see the layout rules in §4).
    Big vertical spans (over 600 px) compress further. Keep the tallest tier under about 14 m above base.
  - The 2D vertical bands become **depth bands**: ground band in front, mid band in the middle, upper band at the
    back.

## 4. The API (`m` is a `MapCtx`; all coordinates are local)

Terrain (build this first; everything placed later is grounded by raycast onto it):

| call | what it makes |
|---|---|
| `m.terrace(name, outline, top, bottom=None, style="grass_dirt", fringe=None, walkable=True)` | Maple platform: a rounded grass cap overhanging an earthen/stone body. `outline` is a list of `(x, y)` (CCW or CW). `style` is one of `grass_dirt`, `grass_stone` (Amherst grey-blue stone), `grass_sand` (Southperry beige), `grass_rock` (warm grey rock landmarks: shaped body, see below), `cobble` (paved plaza), `sandstone` (paved beige), `beach`, `dirt`, `wood_floor`. `stones=False` turns off the automatic cliff stones. |
| `m.ramp(name, (x0,y0,z0), (x1,y1,z1), width, style="grass_dirt", top_mat="M_Path_Dirt")` | Sloped dirt trail with solid sides. Keep the slope at or below 30° (run at least 1.8× rise). The upper end must reach **inside** the upper terrace edge by about 0.5 m. `top_mat=None` (or `"M_Grass"`) makes a grassy slope: it gets the terrace cap's rounded grass lip and hanging fringe along both sides and is lit like the flat caps around it. |
| `m.stairs(name, p0, p1, width, mat="M_Plank", side_mat="M_Wood_Dark", fit_edge=False)` | Steps from p0 (bottom) to p1 (top). Use stairs for steep short climbs (rise of 0.8–4 m). Use `mat="M_StoneWall", side_mat="M_Rock", stringers=False` for stone stairs. The top landing is skipped when the upper cap is already at `p1.z` (±0.05 m) 0.3 m past p1; otherwise it is a flush 3 cm slab in the material of the surface it reaches (grass, cobble...). So end the flight with p1 just inside the cap at the cap's height (or use `fit_edge=True`). `fit_edge=True` slides the flight along its climb direction (run and heights kept) so the top tread sits just inside the upper cap's real wobbled edge, so no lower tread hides under the cap lip as a grass band. |
| `m.ladder(name, x, y, z0, z1, facing=90, kind="ladder"/"rope"/"vine", descend_only=False)` | A climbable ladder, rope or vine against a cliff face. Place it 0.3 m in front of the face; `facing=90` means the climber faces +y. A rope hangs **plumb about 0.12 m off the face** below the upper cap's lip (the face is found by probing toward the cap, so `(x, y)` may be up to ~1.5 m out), with darker knot bands every 0.4 m and an ink outline; it ends at the lip edge in a dark whipping knot (nothing curls over the lip), with a flat peg on the cap behind it. `kind="vine"` or `descend_only=True` gives a leafy green vine hung the same way from a leaf clump. The climb position the validator uses stays exactly `(x, y)`. |
| `m.bridge(name, (x,y,z), (x,y,z), width=2.6)` | Plank bridge with rope rails (walkable). |
| `m.deck(name, outline, top, post_bottom=None, rail=False)` | Wooden platform on posts (docks, walkways, scaffolds). |
| `m.path(name, pts, width=2.6, mat="M_Path_Dirt", end_taper=0.55)` | Painted path draped over the ground. Use `mat="M_Cobble_Path"` or `"M_Sand_Path"` in towns. The through-route of Maple Road / Rainbow Street uses `m.path("Main_Road", pts, **MAIN_ROAD)` (pale cream, 3.6 m; see below). `end_taper` is the width factor at both tips; `1.0` gives square ends (for example to run dirt neatly onto a stair landing). |
| `m.collider(name, boxes=[(x0,y0,z0,x1,y1,z1)], cylinders=[(cx,cy,r,z0,z1)])` | Invisible walkable collision geometry (interior perches, lids and humps under a visual prop). Note the box order: `z0` comes before `x1` (unlike kit `interior.solid`). It joins the ground (walkable, grounds props), never renders, is tagged `obj["collider"] = True` and lives in the `<slug>_Collision` collection. Use it instead of hand-made `hide_render` proxies. |
| `m.water(name, outline, level)` | Flat water with a foam rim (ponds, harbor water). |
| `m.room(x0, y0, x1, y1, wall_h=4.2, ...)` | **Interiors only:** cutaway room (floor + back wall + side walls), with optional windows and a back door. |

Dressing (grounded automatically; `place` raises an error if there is no ground under the point):

| call | notes |
|---|---|
| `m.place(asset, x, y, rot=0, scale=1, z=None, required=True)` | `rot` is a local yaw in degrees; assets face −y at `rot=0`. `scale` may be a tuple. |
| `m.scatter([assets], area, count, seed, min_dist=1.5, avoid=[...], z_band=None)` | `area` and `avoid` are polygons or `(cx, cy, r)` circles. Use `avoid` to keep routes clear. |
| `m.row(asset, p0, p1, spacing)`, `m.fence(pts, asset="Fence_Wood_Seg")` | Lines of props and fences. |

Gameplay:

| call | notes |
|---|---|
| `m.edge_portal(name)` | Places a layout-anchored exit. The target map and portal are looked up automatically. Call it once for **every anchor** of your map. An arrival-only anchor (`layout.ONE_WAY`, today only 40000 `in00`) becomes an **arrival marker** instead (`m.arrival_point`, an Empty with `arrival_name`, no portal object), and the portal that leads to it gets `one_way = True` and `to_arrival = <anchor>` (its `to_portal` keeps the anchor name, Iteration One's v8 target). |
| `m.door_portal(name, x, y, rot=0)` | A door portal into an interior, or back out of an interior. Use the classic portal names. |
| `m.npc(name, x, y, rot=0)` | An NPC proxy whose appearance comes from `src/misostory/npcs.py`. The name must be in `npcs.NPCS`, which follows **MeowDB MS Classic** and carries each NPC's MeowDB id (`meowdb`, a string); the proxy gets `npc_name`, `meowdb_npc_id` and the GMS reference id `npc_id`. The 21 Maple Island NPCs: Heena, Sera, Roger, Nina, Sen, Peter, Todd, Robin, Sam, Maria, Mai, Biggs, Yoona, Bari, Shanks, Pan, Lucas, Pio, Rain, Sid, Lucy. Place each on its MeowDB map (`game/data/canonical/maps/<id>.json` `npcs`), in MeowDB's left-to-right order. |
| `m.mobs(mob, area, count, seed)` | Representative monsters: `Snail`, `Blue Snail`, `Red Snail`, `Shroom`, `Orange Mushroom`. Use **only** the species MeowDB lists for your map (`game/data/canonical/maps/<id>.json` `monsters`). Use roughly one proxy per 4–6 spawn points, at least 6 in a hunting map, spread over the tiers that have spawns. The area (with its `z_band`) is also recorded as an authored **spawn region** tagged with the species (`m.spawn_region`); the proxies are placeholders, the runtime spawns MeowDB's species and counts inside the regions. |
| `m.spawn_region(name, area, species=(), z_band=None, tiers=())` | An authored monster spawn area (local polygon or `(cx, cy, r)` circle) on walkable ground, without proxies: use it where MeowDB spawns monsters the kit has no proxy for (30000's Tutorial Jr. Sentinels). `z_band` (local) picks one tier where the area overlaps several; `tiers` may name struct.json tier ids. Regions with the same polygon and band merge. Exported as `spawn_regions` (polygons). |
| `m.player_spawn(x, y, rot=0, index=0, note="")` | **Required, index 0 on every map.** An authored player spawn (respawns, new games and spawn-index arrivals use it): place it on a route near MeowDB's `player_spawns[0]` px position relative to the portals and NPCs (which tier, which side), at least 2.5 m from edge portals; on the 40000 arrival map, the landing below the one-way ledge; in interiors, 1.6–1.8 m inside the door. An Empty in `CHARACTER_PROXIES/PlayerSpawns_<slug>`, exported as `player_spawns`. |
| `m.route(name, pts)` | **Required.** A walk polyline the validator follows over the real geometry. Declare the main route (anchor to anchor through the map) and routes to every important sub-area or tier. Put route points on the actual walkable path: through ramps and stairs, past ladders. |
| `m.player(x, y, rot)` | The player scale reference, one per map. **Required on exterior maps, and it must be inside the Gameplay render** (`PLAYER_IN_FRAME`, below): put it on a route point near `GAMEPLAY_FOCUS`. It is not a spawn point (that is `m.player_spawn`). |

Useful extras: `m.anchor(name)` gives the anchor's `(x, y, z)`; `m.ground_z(x, y)` gives the current ground
height; `m.size` is `(L, D)`.

### Wave-4 framework additions (iteration-2 review)

- **Main road.** `from misostory.mapkit import MAIN_ROAD` (it is `dict(mat="M_Main_Road", width=3.6)`), then
  `m.path("Main_Road", pts, **MAIN_ROAD)` for the ribbon that carries the island's through-road across your map
  (anchor to anchor). Override the width if needed: `m.path("Main_Road", pts, **{**MAIN_ROAD, "width": 3.0})`.
  Side paths stay `M_Path_Dirt`; Amherst's cobble street stays cobble.
- **Overview framing.** A module constant `OVERVIEW_EXTRA = [(x0, y0, x1, y1), ...]` (local boxes) is merged into
  the exterior Overview camera fit, for landmarks that overhang the footprint. Without z the box spans the height of
  the geometry overlapping it; `(x0, y0, x1, y1, z0, z1)` sets it explicitly. Example (60000's ship):
  `OVERVIEW_EXTRA = [(60, -9, 92, 0)]`.
- **Player in the Gameplay frame.** `scripts/build_map.py` prints
  `PLAYER_IN_FRAME {"ok": ..., "player": ..., "player_uv": [u, v], "mobs_in_frame": n, "mobs_required": k,
  "issues": [...], "player_local": [x, y], "focus": [fx, fy], "scale": s, ...}` and `PLAYER_IN_FRAME_OK True/False`.
  The rule: the `Player_Proxy` origin (its feet) projected through `CAM_<slug>_Gameplay` must land at
  0.1 ≤ u, v ≤ 0.9 (u = left→right, v = bottom→top of the render); maps of kind `hunting` also need at least 2 mob
  proxies inside the frame; interiors report the player but never fail. The same dict is stored in
  `build_report` (`maps.<id>.gameplay_frame`) and checked by `validate.py` (`player_in_gameplay_frame`). Fix a
  failure by moving `m.player` onto a route point near `GAMEPLAY_FOCUS`, or by shifting `GAMEPLAY_FOCUS` /
  `GAMEPLAY_SCALE`.
- **Route graph.** `MAP_TRAVERSAL` now also proves that the routes connect (see §5): `route_groups`,
  `orphan_routes`, `portal_groups`, `portal_split` and `group_gaps` (where to stitch). A split between portals
  makes `MAP_TRAVERSAL_OK False`.
- **grass_rock.** Rock bodies are no longer straight drums: small rocks (effective radius under 2.5 m) bulge in the
  visible band under the cap and tuck in below it, so stacked drums read as separate stones; larger rocks taper in
  toward the foot. 1-3 `Rock_Cliff_Stone` are set into the camera-facing faces (`stones=False` to skip). The cap,
  its height and its walkable outline are unchanged.
- **Stairs** skip their top landing when the cap is already there (see the table); no more plank pads on grass.
- **Ropes** hang plumb against the face with knot bands and an ink outline, ending in a knot at the lip (see the table). If a map
  drew its own rope copy at the ladder's `(x, y)`, it no longer lines up with the kit rope: delete the copy and use
  `m.ladder(..., kind="rope")`.

### Shared behaviour you can rely on (iteration-1 fixes)

- **Stairs:** treads and the top landing sit 3 cm above `p1.z`, so they never z-fight with the cap. Do not add a
  manual +0.03 any more; existing manual lifts are harmless.
- **Ramps:** the profile is gentler (peak slope about 1.25× the mean), and the flat end extensions sit 2 cm
  proud. Default `segments=10`.
- **Paths:** successive ribbons layer automatically (no overlap z-fighting). Ribbons are sampled every 0.3 m with
  several raycast vertices across the width, so they hug cap bevels and cambered ground. Where the ground across
  a ribbon jumps by about 0.3 m or more (a terrace lip, a ramp side) the ribbon is clipped back to the edge: no
  flat overhanging flaps. A centre-line jump larger than a walkable step (about 0.5 m) splits the ribbon, which
  ends right at the lip and resumes below it; smaller steps, such as a cap lip over a ramp top, stay draped.
  Ends taper softly within the old footprint, so ribbons butted end to end still meet.
- **Placement:** `m.scatter(...)` and `m.mobs(...)` automatically avoid props placed earlier with `m.place`
  (footprint ≥ 1 m). Pass `avoid_props=False` to disable this.
- **Ropes** (`kind="rope"`) are thick and readable, hung plumb against the face and draped over the lip to a stake on
  the upper cap (vines: a leaf clump). The climbable metadata (`climbable`, `climb_z0/z1`, `descend_only`) and the climb position the
  validator uses (the object's bounding-box centre, kept exactly at `(x, y)`) are unchanged. The **Butterfly** asset is visible at map scale.
- **Helpers:** `m.face_point(x, y, dx, dy)` marches to a cliff edge and returns `(ex, ey, z_top, z_below)`, which
  is handy for embedding stones, vines and ropes in faces. `m.view_dir()` is described in §3.
- **Trails between maps** leave each edge portal **perpendicular to its footprint edge** for about 3.5 m (plus
  1.2 m inside the map), then turn toward the neighbour. Keep a 4.5 m-wide corridor clear directly in front
  of every edge portal, out to the footprint edge.
- **Trails stay clear of the runtime's containment rings.** Every map is walled at its footprint + 1 m by a 0.5 m
  wall (square corners), open only at its gates (`presentation.json` `containment`), so a trail's centreline must
  keep its half-width (1.9 m on a 3.8 m trail) from that wall, i.e. half-width + 1.5 m from every footprint, except
  where it leaves straight through its own gate. `validate.py` `trail_ring_band` measures it (high when a wall stands
  across the trail). Two maps closer than about 7 m leave no room for a trail between them: move the map, not the
  trail. Where the seeded curve finds no room, author the route in `layout.TRAIL_ROUTES` (corners and fillet radii in
  the from-map's frame; today only Townstreet `west00` → Snail Field of Flowers `east00`).
- **Route clearing:** only instances created by `m.scatter` (and `m.mobs`) that end up on a route are
  removed automatically (natural clutter only). Props placed with `m.place` are **never deleted**; if one
  blocks a route it is reported as `blocked`, so move it.
- **Portal reachability:** every edge and door portal must be reached by a declared route. Some route point has
  to come within 1.5 m of the portal, on ground within ±0.45 m of the portal's height. Otherwise the map fails
  with `portal_unreachable`.
- **Both directions:** routes are walked forward **and** backward. A route containing a jump-down drop must be
  declared `m.route(name, pts, one_way=True)`. Steep pieces and steps covered by a ladder or rope count as
  climbs. Vines that only lead down into a pit: `m.ladder(..., descend_only=True)` (no foot landing needed;
  they cannot be climbed up).
- **Ramps** blend into both terraces with dirt ribbons (`blend=True` by default). Each starts on the ramp surface at
  the ramp's full width, covers the flat end plate and the cap lip band the ramp tucks under, then narrows and
  rounds off on the terrace, so ramp, blend and cap read as one outline. **Stairs** are solid
  blocks.
- **Portals:** every MeowDB portal is a `Portal_Swirl` (edges) or a `Portal_Door`. MeowDB makes the `in00` portals
  of 10000/20000/30000 ordinary two-way exits; only 40000 `in00` stays arrival-only (`layout.ONE_WAY`): it is an
  arrival marker with no portal object and no visual. Door portals are a flat threshold glow with a small chevron
  above, so nothing blocks the door.
- **One-way trails** (`layout.ONE_WAY_LEDGE`, today 30000 `out00` → 40000): the trail ends on the arrival map's
  **gate plane** (footprint + `layout.GATE_OFFSET_M` = 1 m along the anchor's outward normal) in a drop-down ledge
  (2.4 m above the landing) with a rock face (`<trail>_LedgeFace_Body` plus `Rock_Cliff_Stone` boulders). The
  arrival map must have walkable ground right up to and under the plane (40000's `ArrivalApron`), and **nothing
  that could be a step back up** within reach of the face (no boulders, crates or ledges at its foot). The trail
  walk proves forward ok, reverse blocked at the face, and measures the face's offset from the plane.
- **Ground and ink outlines:** the ground index ignores kit ink outlines (flipped-normal `SOLIDIFY` shells from
  `kit.add_outline`), so an outlined walkable mesh stays walkable. Any other modifier is evaluated as usual.
- **Cleared clutter:** `scripts/build_map.py` prints `MAP_CLEARED` (also `cleared_from_routes` in
  `MAP_TRAVERSAL`): the scattered instances removed because they stood on a route.
- **Validator:** routes may include ladder and rope climbs (the slope window stops at climbs), so a route can walk
  up to a ladder, climb it and carry on: no need to start a separate route at every cliff. It also reports props
  blocking a route (`blocked`: geometry inside the body band `traversal.BODY_Z` = 0.15–1.5 m above the walk
  surface; props beside a ladder/rope climb are exempt, see §5) and ladders or ropes without ground at their foot
  or top.

### Layout rules from the island review

- Vertical scale: about 0.55–0.7 m per 60 px tier, with the tallest tier at most about 14 m above base.
- Stair orientation: flights that climb along ±x relative to the camera's view direction (`m.view_dir()`) show
  their risers edge-on and read as loose paving tiles. Where a stair must read as a stair, make the flight climb
  toward +y (risers facing the camera), or use a grass slope (`m.ramp(..., top_mat=None)`) with a painted path.
- Keep `m.place` props at least 1.5 m from route centrelines, and keep a 4.5 m-wide corridor free of
  architecture from every edge portal out to the footprint edge (the trail arrives there).
- Do not scale buildings with doors below 0.8. Stay inside the footprint: nothing may overhang the edge by more
  than about 1 m.
- No map module may import another map module; shared helpers belong in the kit.

## 5. Traversal rules (the validator enforces these)

- Walking steps are at most 0.45 m. A larger rise needs stairs, a ramp, or a ladder/rope within 2 m.
- Slopes are at most 38° measured over 1.2 m.
- Drops of up to 2.6 m are allowed but one-way. Avoid drops on main routes.
- Every anchor portal must stand on ground, and every declared route must have ground everywhere.
- Paths are at least 2.5 m wide, and nothing tall stands on them.
- **Blocked:** an upward ray from every walk sample must not hit a placed prop or architecture inside the body band
  `BODY_Z = (0.15, PLAYER_HEIGHT)` (0.15–1.5 m). Hits on the authored route segment that contains a ladder/rope
  climb, or within 0.8 m of the climb, are exempt when a ladder or rope stands within 2.2 m (a trellis behind a rope,
  dressing on the lip above a ladder foot); they are listed as `climb_exempt` on the route.
- **Props near routes (island validator):** `validate.py`'s `props_near_routes` reports `m.place` props that are
  solid at body height near a route centreline. **Medium** (fails the check): less than 0.45 m body clearance.
  **Low**: 0.45 m up to 1.5 m outdoors (0.8 m in interiors). Hop-able furniture (top under 0.6 m) and archways
  whose opening spans the route are exempt. Read your map's lines in `docs/validation_report.json`.
- **Routes must connect.** Two routes join where a vertex of one lies within **0.75 m** (xy) of a segment of the
  other and the ground there is within **0.45 m** of the vertex's ground height, or where their segments cross. A
  ladder or rope links only routes that walk it (routes whose walks climb the same ladder join); passing near its
  foot or top joins nothing. A portal joins the routes that reach it. `MAP_TRAVERSAL` reports
  `"route_groups": [[route names]]`, `"orphan_routes": [names]` (groups that reach no portal: reported, medium),
  `"portal_groups": [[portal names]]` and `"portal_split"` (non-empty when the portals fall in more than one group:
  **the map fails**). `"group_gaps"` lists, for every group other than the main one, the nearest stitch point:
  `{"group": [...], "portals": [...], "to_main": {"vertex_route": A, "segment_route": B, "from": [x, y],
  "to": [x, y], "gap_xy_m": d, "dz_m": dz}}`. To stitch, add `to` as the first or last point of route A (or bridge
  the gap with a ramp/stairs/ladder when `dz_m` is over 0.45). Example:
  `m.route("Summit", [(27.9, 21.5), (27.9, 23.4), ...])` starts Summit on ArchToCentral.
- Anything the build swallowed (a failed ramp blend ribbon, a failed obstacle index) is listed under
  `"exceptions"` in `MAP_TRAVERSAL` and in `build_report`.
- `scripts/build_map.py` prints `MAP_TRAVERSAL_OK True/False` and the issues. **Your map is not done until it
  prints True** (and `PLAYER_IN_FRAME_OK True`).
- **Island validator, Iteration Two checks** (`scripts/validate.py` on the full build): `fidelity` (NPC proxies,
  by MeowDB id and name, and monster species against MeowDB), `portal_topology` (the 47 MeowDB portals; the one-way
  link's portal and arrival marker), `player_spawns` (`player_spawns[0]` grounded, inside the footprint, its body
  (the 0.3 m grounding capsule of `presentation.json`) outside every portal's 4.5 x 1.6 m press-Up trigger box
  (`portal_adaptations.json`), and on an arrival map at least the gate offset + arrival inset (2.5 m) inside the
  gate plane), `spawn_regions` (every map with MeowDB spawns has regions with walkable ground in their band),
  `trail_walk` (expectations come from `layout`, not the build report; every layout link must be walked; two-way
  trails ok both ways; the one-way trail ok forward with its **only** drop at the ledge, and in reverse blocked by an
  unclimbable step at the face and by **nothing else**), `trail_ring_band` (no trail's centreline within the
  capsule radius of a containment ring wall away from its own gates, none inside another map's ring line; a wall on
  a ribbon's edge is listed as low) and `one_way_ledge` (face on the gate plane within 0.1 m;
  `jump_apex_m_at_cap + step_up_m + 0.30 <= height <= 2.6`, read from `game/data/misostory/movement.json` through
  `src/misostory/ledge_rules.py`).
- **Player spawns must stand clear of prop collision.** The validator cannot see the exported collision hulls; the
  export can. `scripts/export_runtime.py` writes `spawn_clearance` (a 0.3 x 1.5 m capsule against every chunk's prop
  hulls and walls) and `src/misostory/sidecar_diff.py` refuses a staged export with a blocked player spawn. A
  convex hull covers a prop's whole body-height slice, so keep spawns about 1 m from posts, gates and trunks, not
  just off the mesh (000001021's first spawn stood inside the barrel gate's post hull).

### Collision and clearance rules (Iteration Two, enforced by the export)

The runtime export (`scripts/export_runtime.py`) checks the shipped collision, and `src/misostory/sidecar_diff.py`
refuses a staged export that breaks any of these. Run `--check` (below) while authoring; it takes about a minute.

- **Portal arrivals must be clear.** A player arriving through a portal stands 1.5 m inward of its anchor (edge
  portals: along the footprint edge's inward normal; doors: along the door's facing; `portal_adaptations.json`
  `arrival.inset_m`), in a 0.3 m × 1.5 m body. Nothing that collides may stand there: no door mound, hedge, gate post
  or crate within about 1.8 m inward of an edge anchor. 1000000's front-edge barrel-door mounds stand 3.6–3.8 m behind
  their portals for this (`_door(..., back=...)`; the docstring has the arithmetic), and hidden-lane trail hedges
  stop at the footprint edge.
- **Route ends must be clear.** The runtime's route bot starts a run at either end of every route, in the
  controller's body (a floating capsule 0.3 m wide from `step_up_m` 0.45 m to 1.5 m above the feet). End routes at
  least 0.35 m from walls, house stems and gate posts (the walls of a door alcove count), and never under low
  overhead geometry. A route that leads to a portal may stop short of its anchor, but must still end inside the
  press-Up trigger (`portal_adaptations.json` `trigger`: 1.6 m deep, centred on the anchor, so within 0.8 m of
  it along the anchor normal and 2.25 m across): otherwise the route no longer proves the portal is reachable.
  Today's door routes stop 0.4 m short (20000 `ToSensHouse`, 60000 `ArmorStore`).
- **No route under a real overhang.** Route samples under wall geometry inside a walking player's body (above
  `step_up_m`) fail. Lips the feet step onto, and samples on a climb or a drop, are reported but allowed.
- **Keep walks 0.7 m from colliding props.** Props collide as convex prisms of their 0.15–1.6 m slice (a tomato plant
  is a ~0.42 m-radius prism), and a route sample inside one makes the export fall back to the prop's exact mesh,
  which the player then clips. Route centrelines need about 0.7 m from such props' centres plus their radius; walk
  round planted rows, do not cut across them (Tomato Field's `WestBed` and `EastYard`).
- **Collision is data, never a name.** The export decides what collides from flags and tags on the object
  (`misostory.collision_tags`): `walkable` (GroundIndex), `collider`, `climbable`, `passable`, then
  `obj["collision"]` (`ground`, `wall`, `none`, `hull`, `veg_hull`, `water`, `exact`, `overhead_hull`). The kit
  builders tag their meshes (`geo.terrace`: body and cap `wall`, fringe `none`; path ribbons, foam, fringes and sign
  lettering `none`; interior floorboard and flagstone overlays (`interior.plank_floor`, 60001 `flag_floor`) `none`;
  `m.rail`, the ledge face and bridge rails `wall`; water surfaces `water`). A mesh you build yourself into a
  map's Terrain collection (or a plinth or WORLD collection) must be tagged: `CT.tag(obj, CT.WALL)` with
  `from misostory import collision_tags as CT`; `validate.py` (`collision_tags`) fails untagged ones. In an
  Architecture collection an untagged mesh is a wall, so tag a visual-only layer laid over a walkable surface
  (boards, tiles, decals) `none`: as a wall it is a solid slab a few mm above the floor. Kit assets use
  registry tags: any of `collision_tags.PASSABLE_TAGS` (`passable`, `cloud`, `vine`, `flower`, `grass`, ...) makes an
  asset passable, and `collide_exact` makes it collide with its exact mesh instead of a prism (thin overhead
  dressing, such as the interior `Eave_Lashed_4m`, whose prism reached 1.30 m above the ceiling beams).
- **Overhangs above a walking body get their own prism.** A prop's prism covers only its 0.15–1.6 m slice, so a
  cap or roof that overhangs its walls has no collision, and a monster or prop can stand inside it. Tag such parts
  of an asset `CT.tag(part, CT.OVERHEAD_HULL)` in the asset's builder: the export then gives the asset one more
  prism, the convex hull of all its overhead parts from their lowest point to their top, and leaves them out of the
  body slice, so the space under the brim stays open. Only for parts that start well above a body (1.5 m player,
  plus a jump's head room where it matters). Today: 000000030's `X30000_HouseYellowBrim` (`_Cap` and `_Brim`: the
  brim starts 2.8–3 m above the ground; `mushroom_house_v2` returns `(stem, cap)` for this).
- **Checking it:** `"$B" -b MapleIsland.blend --python scripts/export_runtime.py -- --out <dir> --check` writes
  `<dir>/check.json` and prints `CHECK_TOTALS` (blocked spawns, arrivals and route ends, real overhangs, collision
  triangle counts) without baking or writing glbs. Add `--legacy-names` to classify meshes with the pre-registry
  name rules; two check files from one `.blend` then match object by object except where a tag deliberately
  corrects a name rule (today only the 7 interior floor overlays, `none` by tag, `wall` by the old group rule).

## 6. Visual rules

- **Identity first.** Build the landmarks named in the brief and the wiki notes (houses, signs, the ship, the
  knoll, the hump, special trees, gardens). Preserve the tier structure and its rhythm.
- **Gain depth.** Use the extra dimension. Terraces should step back, routes may curve, and sub-areas can wrap
  around a feature you can walk around.
- **Dress generously but deliberately.** Trees and bushes frame the back and sides. Flowers and grass tufts go
  on open grass. Rocks, stumps and logs go at cliff feet. Props tell stories: crates near docks, fences around
  gardens. Keep routes readable and the front edge open. As a rough guide, a hunting map has 10–25 trees and
  15–40 bushes or flowers; a town has 3+ buildings, fences, lamps, signs and benches.
- **Vary the tiers.** Use different outlines (rounded, organic, notched), not stacks of identical rectangles.
  Cobble/stone styles belong in Amherst, beige sand-stone in Southperry, grass+dirt elsewhere.
- Do **not** place debug or marker assets manually; portals, routes and spawns create their own.

## 7. Process (do at least two review cycles)

1. Read your map's brief (`docs/research/maps/<id>_<slug>.md` and `<id>.struct.json`), the wiki notes
   (`docs/research/wiki_maple_road.md` or `wiki_rainbow_street.md`), `docs/research/entities.md` (NPCs and mobs),
   and look at `docs/research/ref_renders/v8_<id>_render.png` (2D reference; never copy it into the project).
   **What** belongs on the map (NPCs, monster species and spawn counts, portals, player spawns) comes from MeowDB
   MS Classic: `game/data/canonical/maps/<meowdb id>.json` (the crosswalk is `game/data/misostory/map_id_crosswalk.json`;
   the v8 differences are in `docs/canonical/topology_diff.md`). The research files are the v8 reference for the
   shape of the map.
2. Sketch the 3D plan (tiers to terraces, ladders, landmarks, routes), then write the module.
3. Build and render:
   ```
   "/c/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe" -b --factory-startup --python scripts/build_map.py -- <MAPID> --out "C:/Users/itech/Coding/maplestory-isometric/renders/_maps/<MAPID>" --debug
   blender -b --factory-startup --python scripts/build_map.py -- <MAPID> --out "$PWD/renders/_maps/<MAPID>" --debug   # Linux
   ```
   It prints `MAP_STATUS`, `MAP_TRAVERSAL` and the rendered file paths. Overview and gameplay views are
   rendered, plus `_debug` versions showing routes (magenta), spawn rings and NPC beacons. Add
   `--extra-cams "x,y,scale;x,y,scale"` for close-ups. Renders share the GPU through a lock and may wait for a
   slot.
4. **Look at the renders** (Read the PNGs) and critique honestly. Does it read as the classic map? Are the routes
   obvious, the landmarks visible, the tiers readable? Is it too empty, too cluttered, floating, intersecting?
   Fix, rebuild and look again. At least two critique-and-fix cycles.
5. Finish only when the build has no errors, `MAP_TRAVERSAL_OK True` and `PLAYER_IN_FRAME_OK True` are printed,
   `orphan_routes` is empty, and the renders look intentional.

## 8. Interiors (20001, 60001, 1010001, 1010003)

These sit on a remote interior stage with no plinth. Build a `m.room(...)` cutaway roughly the size of the
footprint, with floor at `z = 0`. Furnish it from the brief: counters (`Shop_Counter`), shelves (`Shelf_Goods`),
`Weapon_Rack`, `Armor_Stand`, `Rug_Round`, `Table_Round`, `Hanging_Lantern`, `Bed`, `Treasure_Chest` (define
anything else locally if missing). Place the shopkeeper NPC, and put `m.door_portal("out00", x, y)` at the door.
Interiors must still have a `route` (door → counter or NPC). 20001 is the two-storey mushroom-house interior with Sen (a ladder
to a loft). The shared interior helpers (`cut_room`, `shop_frame`, `banded_beam`, `plank_floor`, `solid`,
`crate_block`, `sign_text`, `room_light`, `point_light`, `lbox`/`lcyl`, `ROOM`, `WALL_T`, ...) are in
`misostory.kit.interior`.
For an invisible walkable surface (the top of a pipe hump, a tank lid, a perch on a prop) use `m.collider(...)`
(§4), not a `hide_render` mesh. The interior Overview camera frames the built room (terrain + architecture) plus
15 %, so keep stray geometry out of those collections.
